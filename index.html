Overview

Abstract

Diffusion policies generate multimodal robot action sequences from demonstrations, but steering them toward deployment-time constraints typically relies on differentiable guidance costs. This excludes many practical safety constraints, such as binary collision checks, joint limits, and black-box rollout costs that are nondifferentiable. We propose Gradient-free Robot Action generation via Combined diffusion-MPPI posterior mean Estimation (GRACE), which guides a pretrained diffusion policy with Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control using only forward cost evaluations. Building on the common score-ascent structure of diffusion and MPPI, GRACE constructs a cost-conditioned guidance posterior at each reverse step and estimates its mean with a single MPPI update centered at the diffusion reverse mean. For differentiable costs, GRACE recovers conventional gradient guidance under a first-order, matched-covariance approximation. GRACE attains higher success rates than diffusion-based and sampling-based baselines in simulation. On a real 7-DoF manipulator, GRACE achieves 43/45 successes without obstacle collisions, compared with 17/45 for the unguided prior.

Contributions

  • MPPI-guided posterior-mean diffusion. GRACE integrates MPPI into reverse diffusion by projecting a Boltzmann cost-conditioned posterior onto the Gaussian reverse-kernel family and estimating its mean from MPPI-weighted rollouts, enabling gradient-free adaptation without retraining.
  • Connection to gradient guidance. We show that GRACE recovers conventional one-step gradient guidance for differentiable costs under a first-order approximation with the guidance covariance matched to the diffusion reverse covariance.
  • Experimental validation. GRACE achieves the highest success rates in 2D and 3D simulations, with analyses of trajectory multimodality, guidance sensitivity, and execution horizons, and achieves 43/45 successes without obstacle collisions across three real-robot obstacle conditions.

Guidance on real hardware. The pretrained prior supplies the demonstrated manipulation behavior, while GRACE reweights forward-evaluated rollouts inside each reverse step to shift the posterior mean around an unseen obstacle—without retraining or differentiating the cost or dynamics.

Method

GRACE combines a guidance-independent diffusion prior learned offline with gradient-free, deployment-time cost evaluation. Guidance is applied during the last Ng denoising steps (Ng = 10 in 2D navigation and 5 in 3D manipulation), where the intermediate trajectories are sufficiently denoised for sampled rollouts to provide informative cost evaluations; earlier steps follow the unguided reverse process. At each guided step, the method performs the following posterior-mean update:

  1. Predict the diffusion reverse mean. The pretrained denoiser produces the reverse mean for the current noisy action sequence.
  2. Sample MPPI rollouts. Candidate action sequences are drawn from an exploration kernel centered at the diffusion reverse mean, with a guidance covariance that is controlled independently of the diffusion schedule.
  3. Estimate the guidance posterior mean. The rollouts are evaluated with the deployment-time cost and reweighted to estimate the mean of the cost-conditioned guidance posterior.
  4. Project and sample the next iterate. The estimated mean defines a projected reverse kernel that retains the diffusion-schedule covariance, and the next denoising iterate is sampled from this kernel.

The construction follows from the shared score-ascent form of diffusion and MPPI. Under differentiable costs, a first-order approximation with matched guidance and reverse covariances recovers conventional one-step gradient guidance as a special case.

Simulation Experiments

All diffusion-based baselines use the same UNet prior as GRACE. PO-MPPI applies MPPI refinement after denoising, and CEM-DP, inspired by D-Cubed, propagates the lowest-cost candidate within reverse diffusion; both use the same number of guidance updates and K = 1,000 samples per update as GRACE.

CEM, MPPI, and DA-MPPI remain collision-free but reach only 26.7% success, as their goal-cost-driven proposals favor short, near-straight routes over feasible detours. GG-DP and PO-Grad reach 60.0% but each incurs 12 collision failures, as local gradients can reduce the collision cost without directing trajectories toward feasible passages. PO-MPPI reaches 73.3% with two collision failures: sampling explores alternative detours, but the final diffusion trajectory remains a fixed prior reference throughout post-optimization. Diffusion-ES reaches 23.3% with 23 collision failures, and CEM-DP reaches 50.0% with all 15 failures due to collision, as propagating only the lowest-cost candidate under the shrinking diffusion-schedule variance can steer denoising toward collision-prone routes. GRACE achieves the highest success rate, 83.3% with both backbones, with two collision failures for CNN and one for UNet. The short paths of the diffusion-free planners reflect easier configurations rather than better planning quality, and GRACE's longer paths reflect the detours required to solve harder configurations that other methods fail.

Real-World Experiments

A physical 7-DoF FR3 places a grasped object on a bookshelf while avoiding obstacles introduced near the shelf at deployment time. The diffusion prior is trained on demonstrations collected in a simulated replica of the workspace without these obstacles and is deployed without retraining. Three obstacle conditions are tested: a low obstacle on the left of the shelf (Obstacle 1), a tall obstacle on the right (Obstacle 2), and both together (Obstacle 3). Each trial targets one of the three shelf levels, with the start configuration and goal placement randomly offset laterally, and five trials are run per shelf level under each condition, giving 45 trials each for GRACE (UNet) and the unguided Diffusion Policy (UNet). On hardware, GRACE predicts a 32-step trajectory and triggers the next planning update before the current chunk is exhausted; typically 27 to 28 of the 32 steps are executed before the new trajectory replaces the remainder, an effective replanning rate of about 0.12 Hz.

Additional Analyses

These analyses on the FR3 goal-reaching task accompany our response to the reviewers and are not included in the paper due to the page limit. All methods use the same constraint costs, cost weights, H, and Hexec as the FR3 experiment, and for this comparison GRACE and plain MPPI additionally share K, λ, and the sampling covariance. We report the constraint cost log10(1 + Jc) and the goal cost Jg = ‖qend − qgoal‖ separately. For the diffusion-based methods, an update is one of the last five denoising steps, where GRACE applies guidance; for plain MPPI, an update is one iteration. Plain MPPI runs 16 iterations per plan to match GRACE's per-plan computation time. Plain diffusion is the unguided prior; because it is trained with a fixed 100-step schedule, it cannot be extended to GRACE's budget, so Best of 224, which denoises 224 samples in parallel and keeps the lowest-cost one at each update, is added as a budget-matched reference. Since it uses the cost, it is included only for reference.
